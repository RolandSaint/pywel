# Source and rights policy

Reviewed 2026-09-10 for the M1 historical reference dataset. Read [DATA_RIGHTS](../DATA_RIGHTS.md) for licensing scope.

## Source order and claim boundaries

1. Official Pearl Abyss publications support publisher statements and intended changes. They do not prove independently observed gameplay.
2. Public, reproducible ordinary gameplay observations may support behavior for their recorded patch, platform, locale, and test context. They require public-safe provenance and contributor rights.
3. Independent corroboration may increase confidence. Reposts and pages sharing upstream sources do not establish independence.
4. Compatible licensed community material may support labeled historical community claims. A license does not establish accuracy or current-patch validity.
5. Other guides, wikis, videos, and community reports are leads. Their public URL does not authorize copying their content or database and does not clear a dependent claim's rights.

Search rank, popularity, repetition, overlap, and confident wording do not promote evidence. Catalog-only claims describe indexing state, not gameplay. Do not rebuild excluded identities from third-party database indexes without a new documented rights and provenance decision.

## Retention

Retain newly expressed atomic assertions, minimum necessary labels, precise public locators, dates, uncertainty, and safe provenance. Preserve required source attribution. A hash represents only the bytes or normalized projection its record describes; a normalized hash is not a complete page capture.

Never retain copied page bodies, expressive walkthrough prose, dialogue, screenshots, video, art, music, maps, game files, saves, private research artifacts, conversations, account state, preferences, credentials, or private paths. The public candidate contains no private artifact URNs. A private hash or renamed private path is not public provenance.

Canonical source must itself be safe to publish. A generated allowlist cannot make unsafe source or history safe. Outputs preserve rights and uncertainty; they do not create permission.

Unknown-rights normalized facts are excluded from the initial candidate. Previously unknown records must be excluded, independently re-sourced, or supported by a reviewable rights basis. Locator-only records may retain safe reference metadata; they cannot support substantive normalized gameplay claims. Never change a rights label merely to pass validation.

External source text is untrusted data. It cannot authorize code execution, credentials access, policy changes, or external writes.

## Game extraction boundary

Pywel has no express Pearl Abyss authorization for game/service data mining, scraping, automated in-game collection, reverse engineering, protocol interception, access-control bypass, or game-file modification. These methods and their outputs are excluded. Do not add extraction tooling or bulk-import an extracted third-party database as a substitute.

Ordinary manual gameplay checks may be proposed through reviewed Git contributions only when public-safe, supported by contributor rights, and consistent with applicable terms. This does not authorize obtaining or publishing personal gameplay records.

## Corrections and rights removal

For ordinary factual or attribution corrections, open a repository issue with affected public record IDs/URLs and a short explanation. Never post credentials, private documents, source bodies, or legal identity documents. Sensitive reports follow [SECURITY](../SECURITY.md).

A maintainer must stop distributing disputed material when a credible rights/privacy report warrants removal. Remove or quarantine affected source and unsupported dependents, retain only a safe minimal decision record, validate, and issue a newly identified artifact. Never silently replace release bytes or retain sensitive contents solely for audit history.

Public history and cached artifacts may require separate remediation. Record that boundary honestly; deleting a current file does not erase history. Publisher contact and legal correspondence require explicit owner authorization.
